Ensembl genes and transcripts | Part 1

A hands-on tour of the Ensembl browser: genes, transcripts, regions, variants, and VEP

Author

Isabel Duarte

Published

21 September 2026

Abstract

Five short practicals on the Ensembl genome browser. You will explore a human gene and its transcripts, find a gene from a disease phenotype, navigate and configure a genomic region, interpret population and phenotype data for a SNP, and predict the consequences of a structural variant with the Variant Effect Predictor.

ImportantAttribution and licence

The exercises in this lesson are adapted from the Ensembl browser training material produced by EMBL-EBI and the Ensembl team, which is made available under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence.

Source: Ensembl training materials, https://training.ensembl.org/.

The wording has been reorganised for this course and the questions renumbered. If you redistribute this version, please keep this block and note that changes were made.

Overview

NoteQuestions this lesson answers
  • How do you find a gene in Ensembl and read its Gene tab?
  • What is the difference between all annotated transcripts, protein-coding transcripts, and the MANE Select transcript?
TipLearning objectives

By the end of this session you should be able to:

  1. Search Ensembl by gene name, by phenotype, and by genomic coordinates.
  2. Choose an appropriate transcript for downstream work, and justify the choice.
  3. Turn browser tracks on and off, and export sequence in FASTA format.
Practical details
Time estimate ~1.5 hours (5 exercises, roughly 15 to 20 minutes each)
Level Introductory
Prerequisites Genes, transcripts, exons, UTRs, and the idea of a reference genome
Software needed A web browser only
CautionEnsembl updates

Ensembl publishes a new release several times a year, and annotation changes between releases. Transcript counts, transcript names, identifier versions, and even the set of associated phenotypes may differ from the answers given here. Treat any mismatch as a discussion point rather than a mistake: look at which release you are looking at.

1 Exploring the PCSK9 gene in human

PCSK9 encodes the proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9.

1.1 Locating the gene

Tip🔬 Hands on
  1. Go to https://www.ensembl.org.
  2. Click the Genome selector, type Human in the search box, and click Find.
  3. Select the Reference genome, and click the green Add button.
  4. Type PCSK9 in the Find a feature in the selected genomes search box, and click Find.
  5. Click the PCSK9 gene in the results and choose to View in Genome browser.
  6. Expand the side tab (top blue arrow) to view more information related to the PCSK9 gene: Genomic, Variation, and Regulation. Notice the Eye icon that toggles the View/Hide function for each track.
  7. Explore the genome browser view. Notice that to obtain more information you can click the transcript or the ..., and you can also format the view in the cog icon.
Warning❓ Question
  1. Which chromosome and which strand is the gene on?
  2. How many transcripts (splice variants) are annotated?
  3. How many transcripts are protein coding?
  4. Which is the longest protein-coding transcript (exon length in bp), and how long is the protein it encodes (amino acid length)?
  5. Which transcript would you take forward for further study, and why?
NoteShow answer
  1. Chromosome 1, on the forward strand.
  2. 16 transcripts.
  3. 7 transcripts are protein coding.
  4. The longest transcript is ENST00000713786.2 with 3,852 bp, coding for a 733 aa protein.
  5. ENST00000302118.5 because it is the MANE Select transcript. MANE Select transcripts are agreed between Ensembl and RefSeq (two large databases) as the single representative transcript for a gene, with matching sequences in both databases, which makes results easier to compare across resources. Click on the ? next to the MANE Select transcript for the formal definition.
NoteWhy “longest” and “best” are not the same thing

Note that the longest protein-coding transcript is not the one we choose to work with. Length is a poor proxy for biological relevance. Ask yourself: What would you do if a gene had no MANE Select transcript, and what evidence would look for instead: Expression support, protein evidence, TSL and APPRIS flags in the transcript table, publications, external databases?

1.2 Phenotypes

Tip🔬 Hands on
  1. Click Phenotypes in the left-hand panel.
  2. The table is long. Type MIM in the filter box at the top right to keep only entries from Mendelian Inheritance in Man.
Warning❓ Question

Are any diseases associated with this gene according to MIM?

NoteShow answer

Yes, several. Two examples are deafness (autosomal dominant 17) and macrothrombocytopenia with granulocyte inclusions, with or without nephritis or sensorineural hearing loss. Click any record for more detail.

1.3 Gene Ontology

The Gene Ontology project annotates gene products in three separate namespaces: biological process, cellular component, and molecular function.

Tip🔬 Hands on

Click GO: Biological process in the left-hand panel.

Warning❓ Question

What functions is PCSK9 annotated with?

NoteShow answer

Angiogenesis, cell adhesion, and protein transport are among the biological processes listed. Note that all of the GO terms here are attached to a single transcript, ENST00000216181.

1.4 Inside one transcript

Tip🔬 Hands on
  1. In the transcript table, click the transcript ID for PCSK9-201 (ENST00000216181) to move to the Transcript tab.
  2. Click Exons in the left-hand panel.
  3. Then click General identifiers in the left-hand panel.
Warning❓ Question
  1. How many exons does this transcript have?
  2. Are any exons completely or partially untranslated?
  3. Is there a matching sequence in UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot?
NoteShow answer
  1. 41 exons, as reported in the transcript summary.
  2. Exon 1 is completely untranslated, and exons 2 and 41 are partially untranslated. UTR sequence is shown in orange. The cDNA view in the left-hand panel shows the same thing in sequence form.
  3. Yes: P35579.254 in UniProt/Swiss-Prot matches the translation of this Ensembl transcript. Click the accession to jump to UniProtKB, or click align to see the alignment.

That UniProt accession is the hand-off point between this lesson and the UniProt entry walk-through. If you are teaching both, this is a natural place to switch resources: same protein, different database, different kind of annotation.

1.5 Expression probes

Tip🔬 Hands on

Click Oligo probes in the left-hand panel.

Warning❓ Question

Are there microarray probes that could be used to monitor expression of ENST00000216181?

NoteShow answer

Yes. Probesets from Affymetrix, Agilent, Codelink, Illumina, and Phalanx OneArray map to this transcript, so an expression experiment using any of them would report on this transcript. Expression results for many of these are searchable in ArrayExpress / BioStudies.

2 Finding a gene associated with a phenotype

Phenylketonuria is a genetic disorder in which phenylalanine cannot be metabolised in any body tissue. Phenylalanine accumulates, causing seizures and intellectual disability. Here we work in the opposite direction to the previous exercise: from the disease to the gene.

Tip🔬 Hands on
  1. Go to the Ensembl homepage.
  2. Type phenylketonuria in the search box and click Go.
  3. Narrow the results to Gene using the left-hand menu.
Warning❓ Question

Which gene is associated with this disorder?

NoteShow answer

PAH, phenylalanine hydroxylase, ENSG00000171759.

Tip🔬 Hands on: compare the transcripts
  1. If the transcript table is hidden, click Show transcript table.
  2. Click Transcript comparison in the left-hand menu.
  3. Click Select transcripts, then either tick each protein-coding transcript in turn or use the drop-down and choose Protein coding.
  4. Close the menu to see the comparison.
Warning❓ Question

How many protein-coding transcripts does PAH have?

NoteShow answer

Six.

Tip🔬 Hands on: cross-references and structure
  1. Click External references in the left-hand menu.
  2. Then open the transcript table, click the MANE Select ID (ENST00000553106.6), and choose PDB 3D protein model in the left-hand menu.
Warning❓ Question
  1. What is the MIM gene identifier for PAH?
  2. In the model 2pah, how many protein molecules can you see?
NoteShow answer
  2. Two. The model 2pah is shown by default; rotate it if the second chain is not obvious at first.

3 Exploring a genomic region

This exercise moves from gene-centric to coordinate-centric browsing.

Tip🔬 Hands on: navigate to the region

  1. Go to the Ensembl homepage and select Human.

  2. Type the coordinates in the search box and click Go:

    13:32264000-32492000
Warning❓ Question
  1. On which cytogenetic band does this region sit?
  2. How many contigs make up this part of the assembly?
NoteShow answer
  1. Band q13.1.
  2. Three, shown by the alternating light and dark blue bars in the Contigs track. A contig is a contiguous stretch of sequence assembled from direct sequencing information alone.
Tip🔬 Hands on: zoom in on BRCA2

Drag a box with the mouse around the BRCA2 transcripts, then choose Jump to region from the pop-up menu.

Tip🔬 Hands on: add the LTR repeat track
  1. Click Configure this page in the side menu, or the cog wheel icon at the top left of the lower image.
  2. Go to Repeats in the left-hand menu and select LTR.
  3. Click the (i) button to read the track information.
  4. Save and close with ✓, or by clicking outside the pop-up.
Warning❓ Question
  1. Which tool was used to annotate the LTRs?
  2. How many LTRs fall within BRCA2, and do any overlap exons?
NoteShow answer
  1. RepeatMasker.
  2. Ten LTRs overlap BRCA2, and none of them overlaps an exon.
Tip🔬 Hands on: export the sequence
  1. Click Export data in the side menu.
  2. Leave the defaults (FASTA sequence should already be selected) and click Next.
  3. Click Text.
Warning❓ Question

What information does the FASTA header carry?

NoteShow answer

The assembly, the sequence type, the chromosome, the start and end coordinates, and the strand. For example:

>13_dna:chromosome_chromosome:GRCh38:13:32311910:32405865:1

This is worth dwelling on: coordinates are meaningless without an assembly version, and a FASTA file that does not record one is a liability in any project.

Tip🔬 Hands on: tidy up

Click Configure this page, then Reset configuration, then ✓. This turns off every track you added, so the next exercise starts from the default view.

4 Population genetics and phenotype data

The SNP rs1738074, in the 5’ UTR of the human TAGAP gene, has been reported as a genetic risk factor for several diseases.

Tip🔬 Hands on: find the variant

There is more than one route. Either:

  • open the Variation table for the human TAGAP gene and use the Consequence filter to keep only 5’ UTR variants, or
  • search Ensembl directly for rs1738074.

Once you are on the Variant tab, click Genes and regulation in the menu.

Warning❓ Question

In which transcripts is this SNP found?

NoteShow answer

In four transcripts of TAGAP. It is also an intron variant of one lncRNA transcript of TAGAP-AS1.

Tip🔬 Hands on: allele frequencies

Click Population genetics in the left-hand panel (or click Explore this variant and then the Population genetics icon).

Warning❓ Question

What is the least frequent genotype in the Yoruba (YRI) population from 1000 Genomes phase 3?

NoteShow answer

CC, at a frequency of 5.6%.

Tip🔬 Hands on: conservation
  1. Click Phylogenetic context in the left-hand panel.
  2. Click Select an alignment, open Multiple alignments, choose 91 eutherian mammals EPO-Extended, and click Apply.
Warning❓ Question
  1. What is the ancestral allele, and how was it determined?
  2. Is it conserved across the eutherian mammals?
NoteShow answer
  1. The ancestral allele is T, inferred from the alignment in primates.
  2. The view centres the SNP (highlighted in red) with its flanking sequence. The T allele is conserved in all but two of the eutherian mammals shown.
Tip🔬 Hands on: phenotype associations

Click Phenotype data in the left-hand panel.

Warning❓ Question

Which diseases is this SNP associated with, and are risk alleles reported?

NoteShow answer

Multiple sclerosis, celiac disease, and white blood cell count. Risk alleles and the corresponding P values are given for all three.

Watch the strand. The allele A is reported as associated with celiac disease, and G appears for multiple sclerosis, yet Ensembl lists the alleles for this variant as T/C. Ensembl always reports alleles on the forward strand, so the original publications must have reported theirs on the reverse strand. This is a common and easily missed source of error when combining variant data from different sources.

5 VEP analysis of a structural variant

A breast cancer sample carries a genomic deletion, described here in VCF format:

13  32307062    sv1 .   <DEL>   .   .   SVTYPE=DEL;END=32908738
Tip🔬 Hands on: run VEP
  1. Click VEP at the top of any Ensembl page and open the web interface.
  2. Check that the species is Human.
  3. Give the job a descriptive name, for example Patient deletion exercise. Naming jobs is a good habit: it is how you tell results apart later, and the name is what appears in the browser when you view the data.
  4. Paste the variant into the Paste data field and click Run.
Warning❓ Question

How many genes are affected?

NoteShow answer

Twelve.

Tip🔬 Hands on: filter the results

At the top of the results table, add the filter Consequence is transcript_ablation and click Add.

Warning❓ Question

Does the deletion remove any complete transcripts?

NoteShow answer

Yes. Complete transcripts are lost for PDS5B, N4BP2L1, BRCA2, RNY1P4, IFIT1P1, ATP8A2P2, N4BP2L2, and N4BP2L2-IT2, plus one gene with no official symbol, ENSG00000212293.

Tip🔬 Hands on: view it in the browser

Click the location link in the results table, 13:32307062-32908738. This opens Region in detail in a new tab. If you named the job, the data appears automatically in red. If not, use Configure this page and add it under the Personal data tab.

Warning❓ Question

You saw part of this region already in Section 3. What does the deletion do to BRCA2, and why might that matter in a breast cancer sample?

NoteShow answer

The deletion spans the whole of BRCA2, so the gene is lost entirely rather than altered. BRCA2 is a tumour suppressor involved in homologous recombination repair, so complete loss is a far more consequential event than a single missense change, and it is the kind of variant that a SNV-focused pipeline can easily miss.

This question is an addition to the original exercise set; drop it if you are short of time.

6 Optional: the same queries without the browser

NoteEnsembl REST API and biomaRt

Everything above is clickable, but none of it has to be. The same annotation is available programmatically, which is how you would do this for 500 genes rather than one.

# gene record, with transcripts expanded
curl -s 'https://rest.ensembl.org/lookup/symbol/homo_sapiens/MYH9?expand=1' \
  -H 'Content-type:application/json'

# consequences of a known variant
curl -s 'https://rest.ensembl.org/vep/human/id/rs1738074' \
  -H 'Content-type:application/json'
library(biomaRt)

mart <- useEnsembl(biomart = "genes", dataset = "hsapiens_gene_ensembl")

getBM(
  attributes = c("ensembl_transcript_id", "transcript_biotype",
                 "transcript_length", "transcript_mane_select"),
  filters    = "hgnc_symbol",
  values     = "PCSK9",
  mart       = mart
)
import requests

r = requests.get(
    "https://rest.ensembl.org/lookup/symbol/homo_sapiens/PCSK9",
    params={"expand": 1},
    headers={"Content-Type": "application/json"},
    timeout=30,
)
r.raise_for_status()
gene = r.json()

print(gene["seq_region_name"], gene["strand"])
print(len(gene["Transcript"]), "transcripts")

Both chunks are set to eval: false so the site renders without a network connection. Set them to true if you want live output, and be aware that the numbers may then disagree with the answers above whenever Ensembl updates.

Key points

TipTake home messages
  • Ensembl can be entered from three directions: a gene name, a phenotype, or a set of coordinates. All three converge on the same annotation.
  • A gene usually has many transcripts. Pick one deliberately, and prefer MANE Select where it exists.
  • Left-hand panel links (Phenotypes, GO, Exons, General identifiers, Oligo probes) are where the cross-references live.
  • Genomic coordinates mean nothing without an assembly version, and alleles mean nothing without a strand.
  • VEP scales the same reasoning up from single bases to structural variants, and its consequence filters are how you find the events that matter.

Exercises to extend the session

NoteSuggested follow-up (edit or remove these for your own course)
  1. Repeat the first exercise for a gene of your own choosing and report the transcript you would take forward, with reasons.
  2. Take P35579 from the PCSK9 General identifiers page into the UniProt lesson and compare what each database tells you about the same molecule.
  3. Use BioMart to export the exon coordinates of all protein-coding transcripts of PAH as a TSV file.
  4. Construct a VCF line for a deletion of your own and predict, before running VEP, which consequence terms it should return.
